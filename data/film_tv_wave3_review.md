## Notable Strengths

- FTV-45 Polish crime and FTV-51 Inuit survival: strong grounded cold-weather production realism.
- FTV-46 Italian family saga: avoids gangster cliché and focuses on ensemble blocking.
- FTV-48 Portuguese fado and FTV-57 Cuban music: strong practical interior lighting and musician staging.
- FTV-54 Chile mining board and FTV-73 Kenyan tech board: useful multi-tile production references.
- FTV-63 Hong Kong storyboard: strong action paneling and continuity.
- FTV-67 Uzbek historical set extension: strong worldbuilding and marketplace scale.
- FTV-68 to FTV-72: adds meaningful South Asian, Bangladeshi, Ethiopian, Moroccan, and South African coverage.

## Remaining Risks

- Wan2.7-Image continued adding small labels in FTV-60 after a no-text rerun. Costume/material sheets may need manual cleanup or image editing.
- Several multilingual title posters look plausible but require native-language proofreading.
- Some regional scenes may still benefit from local consultant review before customer-facing use, especially Indigenous or historical topics.

## Recommendation

Keep wave 3 in the gallery. It adds the long-tail and production-chain richness missing from the first two waves. For demos, emphasize the no-text stills, boards, and worldbuilding frames as strongest production examples, and explicitly frame title-bearing images as multilingual typography stress tests.
