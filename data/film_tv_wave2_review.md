# Film & TV Wave 2 Review

## Scope

- 30 additional film and TV cases, FTV-15 through FTV-44.
- 2 models tested: Qwen-Image-2.0-Pro and Wan2.7-Image.
- 60 generated images added to the gallery.
- Main prompt language: English.
- Multilingual text stress tests: Turkish, Thai, Tagalog, Spanish, Arabic, plus English poster/key-art titles.
- Expanded coverage: Turkish dizi, Nollywood, Bollywood, Thai BL, Indonesian horror, Filipino rom-com, Nordic noir, French auteur drama, German sci-fi, Spanish heist, Brazilian urban drama, Mexican mystery, Native American contemporary drama, Australian Aboriginal mystery, Māori fantasy, Caribbean pirate drama, Gulf drama, Israeli political thriller, Egyptian epic, Persian romance, queer coming-of-age, preschool animation, stop-motion, fantasy, orbital rescue drama, post-apocalyptic props, docudrama, cooking competition, music competition, and true-crime key art.

## Overall Findings

| Area | Qwen-Image-2.0-Pro | Wan2.7-Image | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Regional visual authenticity | 4.2 | 4.1 | Both produced stronger diversity than wave 1, especially for live-action settings and ensemble posters. |
| Production asset usefulness | 4.3 | 4.2 | Prop, costume, lighting, set, and VFX boards are generally usable for ideation. |
| Live-action realism | 4.5 | 4.4 | Strongest category in wave 2: French, Indonesian, Brazilian, courtroom, and outback scenes are solid. |
| Animation/design sheets | 4.2 | 3.9 | Wan tends to add labels on some concept-sheet formats; Qwen stayed cleaner. |
| Multilingual exact text | 3.4 | 2.8 | Turkish, Thai, Tagalog, Spanish, and Arabic titles are useful tests but need proofreading and final typography replacement. |
| Production readiness | 4.1 | 4.0 | Good for art direction, pitch decks, and pre-production boards; typography should remain a separate layer. |

## Notable Strengths

- FTV-19 Indonesian horror: both models created restrained, practical-location horror rather than cheap jump-scare imagery.
- FTV-22 French drama: strong natural-light realism and believable social-drama blocking.
- FTV-23 German sci-fi: convincing grounded transit VFX plate without overblown cyberpunk styling.
- FTV-27 and FTV-28: useful contemporary Indigenous drama/procedural frames with respectful modern treatment.
- FTV-30 Caribbean pirate lighting board: good example of same-scene lighting variation.
- FTV-34 and FTV-40: strong costume/prop material studies for art department workflows.
- FTV-42 and FTV-43: good broadcast/reality promo compositions with clean negative space.
- FTV-44: after rerun, Wan produced a cleaner object-only key-art frame.

## Remaining Risks

- Exact multilingual titles remain unstable. Examples include Turkish, Thai, Spanish, and Arabic variants that are close but not always exact.
- Wan2.7-Image still added labels to FTV-29 after a no-text rerun, so concept-sheet prompts may need post-cleanup or a stronger editing workflow.
- Some poster cases add small extra typography even when the prompt asks for only one title. These should be treated as model capability tests, not final release artwork.
- Production teams should continue to overlay all final titles, subtitles, credits, logos, and legal copy in design/compositing tools.

## Recommendation

Keep the expanded set in the gallery because it now shows breadth across region, race, genre, and production chain. For public demos, position the text-heavy outputs as multilingual stress tests and highlight the no-text production assets as the strongest production-ready examples.
